Asexuality and Aromanticism: Details First

What this is

I keep having to explain the basics of asexuality to folks and figured it would be helpful to just have a fast dictionary of terms and details that one could then use to get a quick idea of what asexuality and aromanticism actually are.

The hope is that concepts explained clearly will make things easier to understand, and with that clear foundation you can then build up to bigger concepts.

I think in this manner because I'm Autistic, this is meant to target that style of thought, but isn't exclusive to us, hopefully it's sensible too to non-autistic and neurotypical folks.

The concepts here are intentionally kept at a low depth. If you need more information, please consult wikis that would provide a full set of information.

The raw basics

  • Typical/Atypical: This term is used on this page to specify experiences that the majority of people in society experience, and is accepted by society as being "the usual" experience. Atypical is its opposite.
  • Attraction: This is when you experience an emotional draw to another person over some characteristic they exhibit. There's many different types of attraction. The idea is that based off of some other real-life person's characteristics, you are drawn to that person over a desire to experience them in XYZ manner. You can feel this either immediately or as you get to know them.
  • Sexual attraction: An attraction type. The XYZ here is sex. You are experiencing attraction to the other person and you want to experience them further through having sex.
  • Romance: a structured, formalized and ritualized relationship with someone to meet societal expectations of a relationship. This is not love in itself. Several of the rituals involve dating, marriage proposal, taking on a spouse's name, marriage/vows of commitment and/or exclusivity, family relations, etc.
  • Romantic attraction: An attraction type. The XYZ here is romance. You are experiencing attraction to the other person and you want to experience them further through romance.
  • Asexuality: You do not experience sexual attraction, or you only experience it very little, or you only experience it through highly specific, atypical circumstances, toward any gender. You may still experience other attraction types toward one or more genders.
  • Aromanticism: You do not experience romantic attraction, or you only experience it very little, or you only experience it through highly specific, atypical circumstances, toward any gender. You may still experience other attraction types toward one or more genders.
  • Aroace: You experience both asexuality and aromanticism.
  • Allo- microlabel: The opposite of A-. For allosexual, you experience typical sexual attraction when attracted to someone. For alloromantic, you experience typical romantic attraction when attracted to someone.
